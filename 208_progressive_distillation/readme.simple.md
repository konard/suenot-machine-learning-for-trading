# Progressive Distillation — Explained Simply

Imagine you're summarizing a really long book. First you write a 10-page summary, then a 5-page summary of that, then a 1-page summary. Each time you keep only the most important ideas. That's progressive distillation!

## What's happening?

You have a really big, smart computer brain that's great at predicting things — like whether a price will go up or down. But this big brain is slow and needs a lot of power.

You want a tiny brain that's fast, but still pretty smart.

## Why not just make a tiny brain directly?

If you try to teach a tiny brain everything the big brain knows all at once, it's like trying to pour a whole bucket of water into a tiny cup — most of it spills out!

## The progressive trick

Instead, you do it step by step:

1. **Big brain** teaches a **medium brain** — the medium brain can handle this because it's only a little bit smaller
2. **Medium brain** teaches a **small brain** — again, just a small step down
3. **Small brain** teaches a **tiny brain** — one more small step

Each step is easy! And the tiny brain at the end is much smarter than if you had tried to teach it directly from the big brain.

## In trading

Think of it like a team:
- **The researcher** (big brain) studies everything overnight — all the data, all the patterns
- **The manager** (medium brain) learns the key insights from the researcher
- **The trader** (small brain) gets quick rules from the manager to trade during the day
- **The alarm system** (tiny brain) knows just a few critical signals to watch

Each person in the chain passes down the most important knowledge, and nothing critical gets lost!

## Why it works

Taking small steps is easier than taking one giant leap. When each brain only needs to learn from something just a little bigger than itself, it can do a really good job. The knowledge flows smoothly from biggest to smallest, like water flowing down a gentle staircase instead of a waterfall.
